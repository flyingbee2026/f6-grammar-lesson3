P[Normal|None] [/B]PART B: UPGRADING SENTENCES — MAKING SUGGESTIONS
P[Normal|None] []In the paragraph that suggests solutions, most students write “Schools can …”, “The government can …” and “Parents can …”. These sentences are correct, but they are plain: they show the marker what you think, not how well you can write. Part B gives you THREE patterns that upgrade exactly these sentences. Each one is only ONE clause, easy to remember, and each one carries one “wow” feature that impresses the marker.
P[Normal|None] [/B]Topic for this part:  + []boost the physical fitness and awareness of citizens

P[Normal|None]

  r1 | {27.3} [/B]Level 5: || {142.7} [/B]It is high time that [] the government  [/B]invested [] in free, accessible sports facilities in every district.
P[Normal|None] [/B/C00000]Teaching note:  + [/C00000]“It is high time that” is followed by the PAST tense — invested / launched / reviewed — never the present tense (“invests”). If students panic, the easy alternative is “It is high time for the government TO invest …”.

P[Normal|None] [/B/C00000]Teaching note:  + [/C00000]“make it a priority to” takes the BASE verb (“to add”, never “to adding”). Opening with “To + goal” replaces the vague “so that” and shows the marker your purpose in the first three words.

  r0 | {27.3} [/B]Level 3: || {142.7} []Parents can exercise with their children at weekends.

P[Normal|None] [/B/C00000]Teaching note:  + [/C00000]“in collaboration with” = working together with. It is far stronger than “and” or “also” and shows the marker that you understand shared responsibility. The doer is swappable: “The government, in collaboration with district councils, should …”.
P[Normal|None] [/B/C00000]Teacher’s note on the design:  + [/C00000]the three patterns are deliberately kept to ONE clause. Endings of the “, so as to …” / “, thereby + –ing” type were left out this time — level 3 students lose control of the main clause as soon as they open a second one. Add the endings back only when the class can hold the short form.

P[Normal|None] []Although an extra period for examination preparation sounds attractive, cutting one of our two weekly PE lessons would do F.6 students more harm than good. + [/C00000]  [Topic sentence — states the stance]
P[Normal|None] []To keep us physically fit during the examination year, the school should make it a priority to protect both lessons and to improve what happens in them. + [/C00000]  [Suggestion 1 — today’s Pattern 2]

P[Normal|None] [/B/C00000]What to reward when marking
P[Normal|None] []Content: a clear stance on the cut itself, plus one realistic alternative — not just “PE is good for us”.
P[Normal|None] []Language: the target patterns used accurately; formal verbs (protect, invest in, combat) rather than “help”; no “I think it is good / bad”.
P[Normal|None] []Organisation: topic sentence first, then explain and exemplify, and a closing sentence that returns to the proposal.
P[Normal|None] [/B/C00000]Common errors to watch when marking
P[Normal|None] []“It is high time that the government promote …” → the past tense is needed (“promoted”).
P[Normal|None] []“make it a priority to + –ing” → base verb only (“to add”).
P[Normal|None] []“in collaboration with” used as a verb (“parents collaborate with schools should…”) → keep it as a phrase between the subject and the modal.
P[Normal|None] []Sentence boundaries: a participle phrase after a comma with no matching subject (“Living in a fast-paced society, academic pressure …”), or a new sentence after a comma (“… flexibility, nevertheless, they cannot …”) → full stop and a capital letter.
P[Normal|None] []“the elderly” treated as singular (“the elderlies”, “the elderly feels”).
